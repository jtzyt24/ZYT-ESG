Cat 5 General / mixed waste —
General waste to incineration
?
Mixed solid waste from office, operations, or manufacturing — collected by your waste contractor and sent to NEA incineration. Excludes food waste and recyclables (entered separately below).
Singapore incinerates over 90% of its waste. When your general waste is burned, it releases CO₂. The emission factor covers all gases released during incineration of mixed waste. Reducing general waste means less incineration — and lower waste contractor costs.
🗑 1 large bin bag (65L, ~10 kg) of general waste ≈ 10 kgCO₂e · A small office generating 5 bags/day = 50 kgCO₂e/day ≈ 18 tCO₂e/year
SEFR: Waste → Other Waste → Incineration → 1.0017 kgCO₂e/kg · Source: NEA Singapore

Cat 5 Food waste —
Food waste to incineration F&B / hospitality
?
Food scraps, spoilage, and kitchen waste sent to incineration. Food waste has a lower emission factor than general waste at the incinerator — but it represents wasted food cost on top of the disposal cost.
Food waste is Singapore's second largest waste stream. When incinerated, organic matter releases CO₂. The emission factor is lower than general waste because food has a lower carbon content by mass. However, reducing food waste saves both the incineration emissions and the cost of the food itself — making it one of the highest-ROI sustainability actions for F&B businesses.
🍱 A restaurant wasting 20 kg of food per day × 26 days = 520 kg/month ≈ 3.6 tCO₂e/year from disposal alone — not counting the food cost
SEFR: Waste → Food Waste → Incineration → 0.5698 kgCO₂e/kg · Source: NEA Singapore
Food waste is denser than general waste — a 65L bag holds ~25 kg. Estimate by how full your food waste bin is each day.

Cat 5 Plastics & packaging waste —
Plastic packaging & single-use plastics optional
?
Single-use plastic packaging, bags, films, and containers discarded after use. Plastic has the highest emission factor of all waste types due to its petroleum origin.
Plastic is derived from petroleum — when incinerated, it releases more CO₂ per kg than most other waste types. Even small amounts of plastic waste have a disproportionate carbon impact. Switching to compostable or paper packaging is one of the most impactful packaging decisions for F&B businesses.
🛍 1 kg of plastic packaging waste ≈ 2.89 kgCO₂e — nearly 3× more than the same weight of general mixed waste
SEFR: Waste → Plastic Waste → Incineration → 2.8933 kgCO₂e/kg · Source: NEA Singapore

Cat 5 Recyclables — paper, cardboard, metal, glass —
Verified recyclable waste optional
?
Waste separated for recycling and collected by a licensed recycler. Only enter waste that is actually recycled — not what goes into blue bins (most blue bin content ends up incinerated in Singapore).
GHG Protocol allows a near-zero emission factor for verified recycled waste (compared to incineration). However, Singapore's actual recycling rates are low — most material placed in blue bins still ends up at the incinerator after contamination sorting. Only claim the recycling factor if you have a verified collection contract with a licensed recycler (e.g. cardboard baled and collected by a scrap dealer, e-waste through NEA-licensed collectors).
📦 Verified recycled cardboard ≈ near-zero kgCO₂e · Same cardboard incinerated ≈ 1.29 kgCO₂e/kg — recycling makes a significant difference when actually verified
GHG Protocol: verified recycling ≈ 0–0.1 kgCO₂e/kg depending on type · Contrast: incineration 1.29 kgCO₂e/kg (paper), 0.0005 (glass), 0.0005 (metal)
⚠️ Singapore recycling caveat: Only claim the recycling emission factor if you have a verified collection contract with a licensed recycler. Blue bin recycling should not be claimed — contamination rates mean most blue bin content is incinerated. When in doubt, use the incineration factor and note it as conservative.

Cat 1 PUB water consumption —
Potable water from PUB supply
?
Water consumed from Singapore's public supply. Emissions come from the energy used to treat, pump, and distribute water across the island. Your PUB bill (through SP Group) shows consumption in cubic metres.
Every cubic metre of water you use requires energy to pump from reservoirs, treat to drinking standard, and distribute to your premises. PUB has published the emission factor for this process. Water reduction also reduces waterborne tax and water conservation tax — which add significantly to your water bill.
💧 1 m³ = 1,000 litres of water ≈ 0.57 kgCO₂e · Your April 2026 SP bill: 11.8 m³ ≈ 6.7 kgCO₂e for that billing period
SEFR: Water → Potable Water and NEWater → 0.5662 kgCO₂e/m³ · Source: PUB, Singapore · Includes treatment + distribution energy
