?

Scope 3 Inbound freight — goods coming to you —
GHG Protocol: Cat 4 — Upstream transportation
Third-party logistics / freight (inbound) optional
?
Freight your suppliers or a 3PL provider ships to your premises — raw materials, stock, packaging. You don't own the vehicles but you generate the demand.
Upstream transport covers emissions from moving goods to your premises using third-party vehicles. This includes supplier deliveries, import freight, and inbound courier services. You don't control the vehicle, but you create the demand for the trip.
🚛 Moving 1 tonne of goods 100 km by diesel truck ≈ 10 kg CO₂ (GHG Protocol road freight default)

Scope 3 Outbound delivery — goods going to customers —
GHG Protocol: Cat 9 — Downstream transportation
Delivery to customers (3PL or outsourced) optional
?
Freight from your premises to your customers using third-party couriers or delivery services. Separate from inbound freight — this is the downstream leg.
Downstream transport (Cat 9) covers emissions from delivering your goods to customers. This is separate from Cat 4 (inbound) because the responsibility allocation differs — Cat 9 emissions are caused by your sales, not your purchasing.
📦 1 parcel delivered by van ≈ 0.3–0.7 kg CO₂ depending on distance and van load factor
GHG Protocol default: road freight ~0.1 kgCO₂/tonne-km · Last-mile delivery ~0.15–0.25 kgCO₂/km for small van

Why are inbound and outbound separate? GHG Protocol assigns inbound freight (Cat 4) to your purchasing activities, and outbound delivery (Cat 9) to your sales. They appear in different sections of your Sustainability Report. Most buyers' Scope 3 requests focus on Cat 4 from their suppliers — your outbound is their inbound.
Scope 3 Business travel — flights, ground transport, hotels —
GHG Protocol: Cat 6 — Business travel
Business flights optional
?
Flights taken by any staff member for business purposes during the reporting year.
Aviation has a high climate impact per km compared to ground transport. Flying business or first class has a higher footprint than economy due to more space per seat. For Singapore-based businesses, short-haul SEA routes are most common.
✈️ SIN→KUL return economy: ~80 kg CO₂ · SIN→BKK return: ~200 kg · SIN→LHR return: ~2,000 kg per person

Grab, taxi & ground transport (business use) optional
?
Grab, taxi, or car hire taken for business purposes — client visits, airport transfers, site visits. Not personal commuting.
Business ground transport is Scope 3 Cat 6. Grab and taxi rides in Singapore use ICE cars (mostly petrol) — the emission factor per km is similar to a private car. EV Grabs have a lower footprint using Singapore's grid factor.
🚕 1 km by Grab/taxi (ICE) ≈ 0.173 kg CO₂ · 1 km by EV Grab ≈ 0.062 kg CO₂

Personal vehicle — employee-owned, used for business optional
?
Staff who use their own cars for client visits, site surveys, deliveries, or other business activities. Covers sales staff, field technicians, and any employee who submits mileage claims.
When employees use personal vehicles for business, those emissions are Scope 3 Cat 6 — not Scope 1. The company doesn't own the car, but the business activity creates the trip. If you reimburse mileage, the reimbursement records are your best data source.
🚗 A sales executive doing 8 client visits/week at 10 km average ≈ 80 km/week × 48 weeks = 3,840 km/year ≈ 0.66 tCO₂e
