Why refrigerants matter so much
Refrigerant gases are hundreds to thousands of times more potent than CO₂ as greenhouse gases. This potency is measured by GWP (Global Warming Potential) — the higher the number, the more damaging each kilogram leaked.

R-410A GWP 2,088 — 1 kg leaked = 2.1 tonnes CO₂e — equivalent to driving 12,000 km
R-404A GWP 3,922 — 1 kg leaked = 3.9 tonnes CO₂e — equivalent to driving 22,000 km

The good news: fixing a refrigerant leak costs SGD 300–800 and eliminates the emission entirely. This is often the single highest-return action available to an SME.
Scope 1 Air-conditioning — split units, ceiling cassettes, chillers —
Refrigerant top-up (air-conditioning)
?
Every time your air-con technician tops up the gas, refrigerant has leaked. The quantity topped up equals the quantity emitted to atmosphere. This is a Scope 1 fugitive emission.
Refrigerant leaks happen gradually through micro-cracks in copper piping, faulty fittings, and worn seals. Signs of leakage: AC not cooling as well as before, ice forming on the outdoor unit, or hissing sounds. The refrigerant topped up by the technician equals what has leaked — all of which entered the atmosphere.
🌡 1 kg of R-410A leaked ≈ 2.1 tCO₂e — more than 5,000 kWh of electricity from the Singapore grid
SEFR: Greenhouse Gases → Refrigerants → GWP values per IPCC Fifth Assessment Report (AR5)
Step 1 — What refrigerant does your air-conditioning use?
How to find your refrigerant type:
  1. Look at the label on your outdoor condenser unit (the box outside or on the roof). It will say "Refrigerant: R-410A" or similar.
  2. Check your air-con service report — the technician records the refrigerant type when topping up.
  3. Ask your regular air-con contractor — they will know from their records.
  4. If your system was installed before 2010, it's likely R-22. After 2010, most Singapore systems use R-410A. After 2018, newer inverter models may use R-32.

Scope 1 Walk-in cold room & commercial freezer —
Refrigerant top-up (cold room / freezer) optional
?
Walk-in cold rooms, blast freezers, and commercial display chillers. These typically use refrigerants with very high GWP — a small leak has a large carbon impact.
Cold room refrigerants (R-404A is most common in Singapore cold chain) have among the highest GWP of any commercial refrigerant. A 2 kg leak of R-404A generates 7.8 tCO₂e — equivalent to leaving a 1HP air-con running continuously for 17 years.
🧊 1 kg R-404A leaked = 3.9 tCO₂e · Compare: 1 kg R-32 = 0.68 tCO₂e — switch to lower-GWP refrigerant when servicing

Scope 1 Refrigerated vehicles (reefer trucks / vans) —
Refrigerant top-up (reefer vehicles) optional
?
Refrigeration units fitted to delivery vehicles for cold chain logistics. The refrigerant leak calculation is identical to cold rooms — only the system type differs.
Mobile refrigeration units on trucks and vans are subject to higher vibration stress than static cold rooms, often leading to faster seal wear and more frequent top-ups. Singapore's hot climate also means these systems work harder, increasing leak risk.
🚛 A reefer van leaking 1 kg of R-404A per year ≈ 3.9 tCO₂e — more than the van's diesel fuel combustion from 15,000 km of driving
